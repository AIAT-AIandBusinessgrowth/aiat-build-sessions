# Role archetypes: impact, not title

Look at the work your project needs, from trying an idea to keeping the finished tool running. Use five role descriptions to spot a missing job and choose one small thing to do about it.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Prerequisites** | [The adoption ladder](03-adoption-ladder.md) |
| **Time** | ~10 min |
| **Outcome** | Recognise five kinds of project work, describe the two or three you do and identify a gap. |
| **Last verified** | 2026-09-13 |

## Why this matters

An idea needs different work as it develops: trying it, building it for others, simplifying it, improving it and keeping it running. Here, "archetype" means one of those patterns of work. Naming them helps a team notice when nobody is looking after part of the project.

## Do it

### 1. The five archetypes (4 min)

Boris Cherny, who leads Claude Code at Anthropic, described five archetypes he sees in his team in a public post ([post on X, dated Jun 28, 2026](https://x.com/bcherny/status/2071379474277613732), checked 2026-09-13). The descriptions below are paraphrased. The examples outside software are this material's own.

| Archetype | What they do | You recognise it when | Outside software, for example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Prototyper** | Tries new ideas by building first versions, many of which are not released. | Shows a first working example of an idea. | Builds a small quote calculator for their own sales work. |
| **Builder** | Turns a prototype into something real people can rely on. | Thinks about edge cases, security and what happens when it breaks. | Makes the calculator safe for the whole team to use. |
| **Sweeper** | Simplifies, removes what is not needed, makes things faster and clearer. | Removes an unused feature or an unnecessary step. | Replaces three spreadsheet helper columns with one clear step. |
| **Grower** | Improves a working product based on how people actually use it. | Asks users, watches usage, changes the product. | Notices nobody uses the export and redesigns the start page. |
| **Maintainer** | Keeps a mature system secure, reliable, fast and efficient. | Fixes recurring problems and keeps the tool up to date. | Keeps the team's booking tool running every day. |

### 2. Hats, not boxes (2 min)

The same post says people often cover two roles, sometimes three, regardless of their job title. You can do several kinds of work and change your focus as the project changes.

The post also suggests a healthy mix depends on the product's stage:

| Stage of the product | Mostly needs |
|---|---|
| New, still looking for its users | Prototyper, Builder, Sweeper |
| Growing, people use it | Builder, Sweeper, Grower, some Maintainer |
| Mature, widely used | Sweeper, Grower, Maintainer |

### 3. Self-check (4 min)

Use recent examples to answer in a note. Your role mix may change next month.

```text
Date:
1. Look back: which two activities took most of my energy in the last two weeks?
   Which archetype does each one match?
2. The roles I cover today (two or three):
3. My most important project is: new / growing / mature.
   Does my mix of roles fit what that stage needs?
4. The role nobody in my team covers right now:
5. One small thing I could do in that missing role this week:
```

If you are in a group, compare examples with one other person. Decide who could take a small task in any role your team is missing.

## Done when

- [ ] You can name the five archetypes and one sign of each.
- [ ] You wrote down the two or three roles you cover today, with an example.
- [ ] You named one role that is missing in your team or project.

## Data note

When you describe examples for your self-check, describe the kind of work, not real colleagues or customers. Do not rate named people by archetype in any shared tool.
