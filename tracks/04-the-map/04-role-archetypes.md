# Role archetypes: impact, not title

| | |
|---|---|
| **Prerequisites** | [The adoption ladder](03-adoption-ladder.md) |
| **Time** | ~10 min |
| **Outcome** | After this unit you can name the five role archetypes, say which two or three you wear today, and see which role your team or project is missing. |
| **Last verified** | 2026-09-13 |

## Why this matters

When building gets cheap, job titles stop telling you who does what. A designer builds a prototype. An analyst cleans up a messy tool. A project manager keeps a small system running. The useful question is no longer "what is your job title?" but "what kind of impact do you have on the thing being built?"

Archetypes give you words for that. They also help a team notice a gap: plenty of new ideas, but nobody who makes them reliable, or nobody who simplifies.

## Do it

### 1. The five archetypes (4 min)

Boris Cherny, who leads Claude Code at Anthropic, described five archetypes he sees in his team in a public post ([post on X, dated Jun 28, 2026](https://x.com/bcherny/status/2071379474277613732), checked 2026-09-13). The descriptions below are paraphrased. The examples outside software are this material's own.

| Archetype | What they do | You recognise it when | Outside software, for example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Prototyper** | Comes up with new ideas and builds first versions. Most never ship. | Discussions turn into "look, it already runs". | Builds a small quote calculator for their own sales work. |
| **Builder** | Turns a prototype into something real people can rely on. | Thinks about edge cases, security and what happens when it breaks. | Makes the calculator safe for the whole team to use. |
| **Sweeper** | Simplifies, removes what is not needed, makes things faster and cleaner. | Deletes a feature and everyone is relieved. | Replaces three spreadsheet helper columns with one clear step. |
| **Grower** | Improves a working product based on how people actually use it. | Asks users, watches usage, changes the product. | Notices nobody uses the export and redesigns the start page. |
| **Maintainer** | Keeps a mature system secure, reliable, fast and efficient. | "Boring" is the goal. | Keeps the team's booking tool running every day without surprises. |

### 2. Hats, not boxes (2 min)

The same post notes that many people span two roles, sometimes three, and that the roles are not tied to job function. Think of the archetypes as hats. You can wear more than one, and you change hats as a project changes.

The post also suggests a healthy mix depends on the product's stage:

| Stage of the product | Mostly needs |
|---|---|
| New, still looking for its users | Prototyper, Builder, Sweeper |
| Growing, people use it | Builder, Sweeper, Grower, some Maintainer |
| Mature, widely used | Sweeper, Grower, Maintainer |

### 3. Self-check (4 min)

Answer in a note. There is no right answer, and it may change next month.

```text
Date:
1. Look back: which two activities took most of my energy in the last two weeks?
   Which archetype does each one match?
2. The hats I wear today (two or three):
3. My most important project is: new / growing / mature.
   Does my mix of hats fit what that stage needs?
4. The hat nobody in my team wears right now:
5. One small thing I could do in that missing hat this week:
```

If you are in a group, compare hats with one other person. If everyone in your team wears the same hat, decide together who takes one of the missing ones.

## Done when

- [ ] You can name the five archetypes and one sign of each.
- [ ] You wrote down the two or three hats you wear today, with an example.
- [ ] You named one hat that is missing in your team or project.

## Data note

When you describe examples for your self-check, describe the kind of work, not real colleagues or customers. Do not rate named people by archetype in any shared tool.
