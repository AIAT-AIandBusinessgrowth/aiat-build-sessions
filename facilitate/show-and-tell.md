# Show and tell

The round near the end of a session, where a few people show what they built. It takes about ten minutes. It is not a competition and not a code review. It shows the room what is possible with the same tools, one level above where they are today.

## The format

- Three minutes per person or team, with a visible timer.
- Three or four people per round. Everyone else shows next time.
- Each person follows the [show-and-tell card](../templates/show-and-tell-card.md): problem, demo, one thing learned, ladder level.
- One question from the room, then the next person.

## Show the thing, not the code

Scrolling through code loses the room within seconds. Show what the tool does instead:

- The running app or the result, with one click that matters.
- Or a screenshot, or a photo of a paper sketch, labelled "mockup" if it is not built yet.
- Or the prompt that worked, and what came out.

If someone starts scrolling code, ask: "What does that do for the person who uses it?"

## Before the round

- Ask for volunteers during the build block, not at the end. Also ask people who built something small and useful. A modest first build encourages the room more than a polished one.
- Give each person five minutes with the card to prepare.
- Screen check: only fake data on screen. Mail, chat and notifications closed. Browser tabs with real accounts closed.

## During the round

- Keep time. At three minutes, thank the person and move on.
- Ask for the one thing learned if they skip it. That is the part others take home.
- Keep questions kind and short. "How did you check it?" is always a good one.

## Consent for quotes and photos

Quotes and photos are personal data. Use them only with explicit consent, given in writing, for that use.

- **Quotes:** only if the person ticked a box on the [closing card](../templates/closing-card.md): without name, or with first name. No ticked box means no quote, even if they said it out loud.
- **Photos:** ask the room before you take any. Anyone who does not want to be in a photo gets a place outside the frame, without having to explain. Photos of screens show only fake data.
- **Publishing:** before you publish a photo, get a ticked consent box from every person who can be recognised on it. A simple form is enough:

  ```text
  Photo consent
  [ ] You may publish photos from this session on which I can be recognised.
  [ ] Please do not publish photos on which I can be recognised.
  Withdraw at any time by telling the host.
  ```

- **Withdrawal:** if someone withdraws consent, remove the quote or photo wherever you still control it, and do not use it again.
- Store consent cards like any other personal data: only as long as you use the quote or photo, and not in a public repository.
